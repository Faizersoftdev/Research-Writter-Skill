# School sample manuscript: observed formatting and writing conventions

This is a reusable summary of the examples provided in the Hourly conversation, not a claim that every department mandates these details.

## Content organization
- Research-paper/IMRAD headings in capitals: `INTRODUCTION`, `METHODS`; postpone `RESULTS` and `DISCUSSION` if the adviser only requests the first two sections.
- Formal, cohesive paragraphs move **from general to specific**, in both Introduction and Methods.
- Introduction: broad subject → evidence and local setting → problem → related research / reason for proposed solution → system details and benefits → general objective → separate bulleted objectives → scope, limits and beneficiaries. Exact paragraph order may reflect the latest adviser's instruction.
- Objectives appear after an introductory statement, with one specific 'To ...' objective per bullet.
- Methods begins by explaining what the chosen methodology is, **why it suits the system**, what its stages are, and how real project work maps onto every stage.
- Diagram is referenced at the logical transition after introducing stages, **before** stage-by-stage explanation.
- Hardware table and descriptive discussion, followed by software table and descriptive discussion. The prose explains what tools are *for* rather than merely repeating table cells.
- Activity Diagram is summarized in Methods while the full diagram can be located in the appendix at the back of the proposal.
- Software-quality evaluation describes the approved standard and pending procedure. Results must not be invented.

## Visual and citation conventions
- Examples depict a two-column printed layout, justified text, conventional paragraph indentation, figures/tables with descriptive italic captions. Exact typography, layout and numbering depend on the institutional template.
- Sequential numbered **superscripts** with full APA-style bibliographic entries in page footnotes; this is not orthodox APA 7 author–date in-text style.
- Entries are clean citations, not article reviews or annotations.
- Avoid duplicate source entries; reuse the assigned number unless adviser specifies otherwise.
